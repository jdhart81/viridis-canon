# AI use and authorship

Viridis uses automated tools extensively while keeping human responsibility
explicit.

## Accountable author

Justin D. Hart is the accountable human author for Viridis research unless a
specific artifact names another human collaborator who has accepted authorship
and responsibility. Justin is responsible for the claims, references, released
artifacts, and publication decisions.

## Tool roles

- **Aristotle (Harmonic)** is used as an automated Lean theorem prover. Its
  project identifiers, returned artifacts, toolchains, and summaries are
  preserved as verification provenance.
- **Claude** may be used for drafting, synthesis, critique, citation review,
  and post-proof paper reconciliation.
- Other software may be used for numerical checks, catalog generation, and
  reproducibility testing.

Tool output is evidence about a workflow, not independent scholarly
endorsement. Lean acceptance establishes that a proof term inhabits its stated
type; it does not establish that the assumptions describe nature.

## Publication policy

- Automated systems are not listed as journal authors.
- Substantive tool use is disclosed in the paper or acknowledgment appropriate
  to the target venue.
- Git co-author trailers and Aristotle summaries record technical provenance;
  they do not determine scholarly authorship.
- Every public artifact must identify what was machine-checked, what was
  numerically tested, what remains assumed, and what requires empirical
  validation.

Venue-specific disclosures may be more detailed, but they may not weaken this
human-accountability boundary.
