What this is

The Viridis Canon is an open research program that uses AI systems to propose and prove results about decisions in ecology and conservation. Each result is released in three linked forms: Lean 4 proofs built against a pinned Mathlib, a paper with a DOI, and a decision function you can run. The Git repository github.com/jdhart81/viridis-canon is where results are released and corrected; the Zenodo record (concept DOI 10.5281/zenodo.19317982) is the permanent, citable snapshot. Three results

Anytime-valid change detection for ecological monitoring

Has this monitoring signal really changed? And if I re-check after every new observation, for as long as the program runs, how do I keep false alarms under control?

Monitoring programs look at data continuously. Re-running a classical test after every new count inflates the false-alarm rate. This result gives a monitor whose probability of ever raising a false alarm is at most α, however long you watch and however often you look.

It also tells you what a late change costs (a log-sized evidence penalty) and, when the evidence increments are bounded, bounds how long, on average, a real change takes to detect.

  • Ville's inequality. For a non-negative martingale (Mn) and c > 0: P(∃n: Mn ≥ c) ≤ E[M0]/c.
  • The monitor. Mn = An + tn, with A0 = 0, An = Λn(An−1 + πn), change-time prior πk = 1/(k(k+1)) and tail tn = 1/(n+1). Λn is the likelihood ratio of observation n. Under the null it is a non-negative martingale with M0 = 1.
  • Anytime false-alarm control. P0(∃n: Mn ≥ 1/α) ≤ α.
  • Late-change penalty. The monitor dominates every embedded change-at-ν detector and alarms no later than it; the evidence boundary for a change at ν is log(1/α) + log(ν(ν+1)).
  • Detection delay. If post-change log-likelihood increments Zj are each independent of the past, uniformly bounded (|Zj| ≤ B), at most zmax, and have mean D > 0, and the first time Na the evidence sum reaches a > 0 has finite expectation, then a/D ≤ E[Na] < (a + zmax)/D.

Corrected 2026-11-13 (bounded_overshoot_expected_delay). Earlier descriptions of this result gave the delay bound for increments “bounded above by z_max”. The certified theorem also assumes a uniform two-sided bound, |Z| ≤ B, so it covers the bounded-increment case of the paper's claim. The paper's next version will state this. Found by @mak777uh (#121).

Can a seed supply network meet restoration demand?

Can our seed sources meet every planting site's demand under our seed-transfer rules? If not, which sites are the bottleneck, and will buying more seed help?

Give the tool your seed lots (with quantities), your planting sites (with demand) and which lots may go to which sites, for example under climate-matched provenance rules. It returns the largest share of every site's demand you can meet at the same time, and the sites that limit it.

Adding seed at a source that cannot reach the bottleneck sites cannot raise that share. Checking several climate scenarios together can only lower it, never raise it.

Having as much seed in total as you need is not enough: the certified negative control shows total supply exactly equal to total demand while only a small fraction of every site's demand can be met.

  • Common fulfilment fraction. θ* = min{1, min over non-empty site sets U of S(N(U)) / D(U)}, where N(U) is the set of sources allowed to serve some site in U, S is total supply and D is total demand. θ* is the largest feasible fraction.
  • Full supply. Every site can be fully supplied if and only if D(U) ≤ S(N(U)) for every non-empty U (a capacitated Hall condition).
  • Bottleneck. When θ* < 1, increasing supply at a source outside N(U) for the active bottleneck U cannot increase θ*.
  • Scenarios. The certificate for transfers allowed in every scenario is at most the certificate of each scenario.

A precautionary capacity rule that is provably safe and provably tight

If I only know the average and the spread of an uncertain capacity, how much can I safely plan to use, with a guaranteed bound on the chance of falling short?

Plan against the floor d = [μ − σ√((1−α)/α)]₊. Whatever the shape of the uncertainty (any non-negative distribution with mean μ and standard deviation σ), the chance that capacity falls below that floor is at most α.

You cannot do better with the mean and variance alone: for any higher floor there is a two-point distribution that breaks the guarantee. Planning on the mean alone can fail with probability above any level below 1.

  • Two-moment floor. dα = [μ − σ√((1−α)/α)]+, from Cantelli's one-sided inequality (proved here; Mathlib at our pinned revision has Chebyshev's but not Cantelli's).
  • Guarantee. P(D < dα) ≤ α for every non-negative capacity D with mean μ and standard deviation σ.
  • Sharpness. For any d > dα > 0 there is a two-point law with the same mean and variance under which the violation probability exceeds α.
  • Mean-only rule. For any β < 1 there is a non-negative two-point law with mean μ under which planning on μ fails with probability above β.
  • In the paper's model the certified rate is rα = P·dα/(kBT ln 2), and the reserve fraction is rα/rμ = [1 − CV·√((1−α)/α)]+.

What a certificate establishes, and what it does not

The listed Lean theorems are proved in the solution file, their statements match the challenge file, they use only the permitted axioms, and the Lean kernel and the nanoda kernel accept them. It does not establish that the formal statement captures the paper's prose claim, that the model describes nature, that the result is new, or that any decision built on it is correct.
