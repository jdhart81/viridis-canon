A precautionary capacity rule that is provably safe and provably tight

Paper: The Precautionary Capacity Reserve: A Sharp Moment-Robust Stewardship Discount for an Intelligence-Rate Model. Justin D. Hart, 2026. DOI 10.5281/zenodo.21971046 · PDF · Lean source · certificate

The question

If I only know the average and the spread of an uncertain capacity, how much can I safely plan to use, with a guaranteed bound on the chance of falling short?

The answer, in plain words

Plan against the floor d = [μ − σ√((1−α)/α)]₊. Whatever the shape of the uncertainty (any non-negative distribution with mean μ and standard deviation σ), the chance that capacity falls below that floor is at most α.

You cannot do better with the mean and variance alone: for any higher floor there is a two-point distribution that breaks the guarantee. Planning on the mean alone can fail with probability above any level below 1.

The mathematics

  • Two-moment floor. dα = [μ − σ√((1−α)/α)]+, from Cantelli's one-sided inequality (proved here; Mathlib at our pinned revision has Chebyshev's but not Cantelli's).
  • Guarantee. P(D < dα) ≤ α for every non-negative capacity D with mean μ and standard deviation σ.
  • Sharpness. For any d > dα > 0 there is a two-point law with the same mean and variance under which the violation probability exceeds α.
  • Mean-only rule. For any β < 1 there is a non-negative two-point law with mean μ under which planning on μ fails with probability above β.
  • In the paper's model the certified rate is rα = P·dα/(kBT ln 2), and the reserve fraction is rα/rμ = [1 − CV·√((1−α)/α)]+.

Assumptions and limits

  • μ and σ are taken as known; estimation error in them is not covered.
  • One period, one marginal distribution; no time dependence.
  • The certified statements are written for the paper's information-rate model; the floor itself is Cantelli's bound and does not depend on that model.
  • Not field-validated: no program has used it yet.
