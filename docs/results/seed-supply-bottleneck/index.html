Can a seed supply network meet restoration demand?

Paper: The Seed-Source Bottleneck Certificate: A cut condition for climate-aligned afforestation supply networks. Justin D. Hart, 2026. DOI 10.5281/zenodo.21971057 · PDF · Lean source · certificate

The question

Can our seed sources meet every planting site's demand under our seed-transfer rules? If not, which sites are the bottleneck, and will buying more seed help?

The answer, in plain words

Give the tool your seed lots (with quantities), your planting sites (with demand) and which lots may go to which sites, for example under climate-matched provenance rules. It returns the largest share of every site's demand you can meet at the same time, and the sites that limit it.

Adding seed at a source that cannot reach the bottleneck sites cannot raise that share. Checking several climate scenarios together can only lower it, never raise it.

Having as much seed in total as you need is not enough: the certified negative control shows total supply exactly equal to total demand while only a small fraction of every site's demand can be met.

The mathematics

  • Common fulfilment fraction. θ* = min{1, min over non-empty site sets U of S(N(U)) / D(U)}, where N(U) is the set of sources allowed to serve some site in U, S is total supply and D is total demand. θ* is the largest feasible fraction.
  • Full supply. Every site can be fully supplied if and only if D(U) ≤ S(N(U)) for every non-empty U (a capacitated Hall condition).
  • Bottleneck. When θ* < 1, increasing supply at a source outside N(U) for the active bottleneck U cannot increase θ*.
  • Scenarios. The certificate for transfers allowed in every scenario is at most the certificate of each scenario.

Assumptions and limits

  • One period, continuous quantities, and a yes/no rule for which source may serve which site.
  • Costs, timing, storage losses and germination are outside the model unless folded into the supply numbers.
  • The underlying theorem is classical (Hall, Gale, max-flow/min-cut). What is new is a machine-checked statement for this decision, built from scratch because Mathlib has no capacitated Hall theorem.
  • Not field-validated: no seed program has used it yet.
