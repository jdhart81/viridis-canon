\begin{abstract}
We study a deliberately classical two-agent decision gate under the AI Wavefunction Collapse and Symbiosis intersection. Two calibrated binary agents independently inspect the same balanced latent state, each with symmetric accuracy $p$, and the system acts only when they agree. Its acting probability is $M(p)=p^2+(1-p)^2$ and its conditional correctness is $C(p)=p^2/M(p)$. The exact gain is $C(p)-p=p(1-p)(2p-1)/M(p)$: agreement postselection helps precisely for an independently competent pair, while a copied-agent control provides no gain by model construction; the frozen Lean theorem named for that control certifies only reflexivity $p=p$. \section{Introduction and relevant prior work}
Multi-agent systems often treat agreement as evidence. That intuition is unsafe unless accuracy, class balance, and error dependence are explicit. The Condorcet-jury literature identifies competence and conditional independence as load-bearing aggregation assumptions \cite{boland1989}. Classifier co-validation uses disagreement between independently trained models as information about prediction error \cite{madani2004}, while ensemble-training work makes class-conditional covariance and diversity explicit \cite{ferrer2009}.

Quantum-probability models of cognition use state-update and contextuality language to model decisions that resist classical probability descriptions \cite{pothos2009}. The present note does not demonstrate such an effect. It uses ordinary conditional probability and borrows ``collapse'' only for the operational transition from two reports to act or abstain.

\section{Model and preregistered question}
Let $Y\in\{0,1\}$ be balanced. Conditional on $Y$, agents $A$ and $B$ have equal symmetric accuracy $p$ and independent errors. The gate acts exactly on $A=B$ and outputs the agreed label. These assumptions imply two mutually exclusive agreement events: both correct, with mass $p^2$, or both wrong, with mass $(1-p)^2$.

The independence assumption is conditional on $Y$; it is not inferred from differently named models, different prompts, or separate processes. The gate's confidence is conditional on acting, so abstentions must be reported separately.

\section{Result}
\textbf{Proposition 1 (agreement-conditioned confidence).} For $0<p<1$, the acting mass and conditional correctness are
\begin{equation}
M(p)=p^2+(1-p)^2,\qquad C(p)=\frac{p^2}{M(p)}.
\label{eq:core}
\end{equation}
Moreover,
\begin{equation}
C(p)-p=\frac{p(1-p)(2p-1)}{M(p)},
\qquad 1-M(p)=2p(1-p).
\label{eq:gain}
\end{equation}

\emph{Proof.} Equation~\eqref{eq:core} is Bayes' rule on the two agreement events. Subtracting $p$ over the common denominator and factoring gives the first identity in Eq.~\eqref{eq:gain}; expanding gives the second. Since $M(p)>0$, the gain sign is the sign of $2p-1$ in the probability interval. \hfill$\square$

\textbf{Corollary 1.} Agreement conditioning is strictly beneficial for $1/2<p<1$, neutral at $p=1/2$ and $p=1$, and harmful for $0<p<1/2$. This is confidence-among-acted-cases, not unconditional task accuracy.

\section{Worked witness and dependence controls}
At $p=3/4$, both-correct mass is $9/16$, both-wrong mass is $1/16$, and disagreement mass is $6/16$. The system acts on $5/8$ of cases and is correct on $9/10$ of those, a conditional gain of $3/20$ over either individual. The unconditional mass of correct acted cases remains $p^2=9/16$; abstention is the price of the higher conditional confidence.

If $B$ copies $A$, agreement is automatic and conditional correctness is only $p$. This copied-agent statement is supported by the declared model and deterministic control, not by a substantive formal copied-agent theorem; the corresponding frozen Lean declaration proves only $p=p$. If errors are perfectly anticorrelated, agreement never occurs. Between these extremes the independence formula is not licensed without a dependence model. A two-agent headcount is therefore not evidence of symbiosis; independently informative error structure is.
