\section{Introduction and prior work}

Hierarchical Dendritic Forest Management (HDFM) represents landscapes as
weighted corridor graphs and explicitly treats its resistance surfaces and
ecological projections as hypotheses requiring field validation.  Current
planning methods go much further than the calculation below.  They jointly
optimize multispecies connectivity under acquisition budgets
\cite{dilkina2017}, route climate connectivity along temperature gradients
\cite{nunez2013}, combine connectivity and species-distribution models across
many taxa \cite{miranda2021}, and union species-specific least-cost corridors
to locate multispecies zones \cite{ersoy2019}. Recent integer-programming work
also encodes corridor geometry, width, size, connectivity, and redundancy
\cite{wang2026}.

This note asks only a preliminary question: if two modeled desired movement
directions are already supplied, what common directional-progress floor can a
single unit heading guarantee?  It contains no resistance calibration, habitat
model, demographic response, land-cost layer, or spatial optimization.  The
triviality probe found a short Cauchy--Schwarz proof, so the generator assigns
\texttt{METHODS\_NOTE} before numerical testing.

\section{Model and assumptions}

Let $u,w$ be unit vectors in a real inner-product space.  They represent two
declared movement-preference or climate-tracking directions, not measured
animal trajectories.  Let $v$ be an oriented unit heading for one corridor
segment.  Define
\[
c=u\cdot w\in[-1,1],\qquad
m(v)=\min\{u\cdot v,w\cdot v\}.
\]
The value $c$ is a compatibility input.  It is not estimated here.  Because a
physical corridor is often bidirectional, the orientation must be tied to the
specific modeled migration or tracking task before interpreting a dot product.

\section{Results}

\paragraph{Proposition 1 (two-direction ceiling).}
For every unit $v$,
\begin{equation}
m(v)\leq M(c):=\sqrt{\frac{1+c}{2}}. \tag{1}
\end{equation}
If $u\neq-w$, equality is attained by
$v_{\rm b}=(u+w)/\lVert u+w\rVert$.

\paragraph{Proof.}
The minimum does not exceed the arithmetic mean, so
\[
m(v)\leq \frac{(u+w)\cdot v}{2}
\leq \frac{\lVert u+w\rVert\lVert v\rVert}{2}
=\sqrt{\frac{1+u\cdot w}{2}}.
\]
For the normalized bisector, both dot products equal the final expression.
\hfill$\square$

\paragraph{Formal-certificate scope.} The frozen Lean declaration for the
ceiling assumes the scalar quadratic premise
$((a+b)/2)^2\leq(1+c)/2$; it certifies the resulting scalar implication,
not the derivation of that premise from unit-vector geometry. The frozen
bisector declaration certifies only $\min\{M(c),M(c)\}=M(c)$. The vector
argument above is therefore an analytical proof outside the formal claim map.

If the separation angle is $\Delta\in[0,\pi]$, then $c=\cos\Delta$ and
$M(c)=\cos(\Delta/2)$. Coincident directions give one; orthogonal directions
give $1/\sqrt{2}$; antipodal directions give zero.  At antipodality the
bisector is undefined, but any unit vector orthogonal to $u$ attains zero.

\paragraph{Proposition 2 (positive-length aggregation).}
Suppose route segments have headings $v_i$, lengths $\ell_i\geq0$, and for
each $i$ both $u\cdot v_i\geq m$ and $w\cdot v_i\geq m$, where $m\geq0$.
For aggregate displacement $S=\sum_i\ell_i v_i$ and total length
$L=\sum_i\ell_i$,
\[
u\cdot S\geq mL,\qquad w\cdot S\geq mL. \tag{2}
\]
This is linearity, not a statement that tortuosity is costless.  It says only
that bending within the declared common-alignment cone preserves the projected
progress certificate.  If a required projected displacement is $D>0$ for
either preference and the certificate is $m>0$, then $L\geq D/m$.

\paragraph{Scope control for three directions.}
Equation (1) is pairwise. Three planar unit directions spaced by 120 degrees
have no positive common heading; a dense search gives maximin value $-1/2$.
Pairwise compatibility therefore cannot be promoted into a multi-taxon
guarantee.

\section{Discussion and limitations}

Equation (1) is best used as an early incompatibility flag.  A low value says
that one oriented heading cannot strongly serve both declared directions; it
does not say whether separate corridors, a branching network, habitat
restoration, stepping stones, or no intervention is preferable.  A high value
does not establish habitat quality, permeability, occupancy, gene flow,
population viability, or resilience.

The model omits uncertainty in preference directions, resistance surfaces,
terrain, width, edge effects, land ownership, disturbance, climate nonstationarity,
species interactions, bidirectional movement, costs, and network redundancy.
Dot products are coordinate-dependent unless the spatial representation is
chosen appropriately. The pairwise certificate does not scale to many species.
No field, customer, or ecological-effect data were analyzed.
