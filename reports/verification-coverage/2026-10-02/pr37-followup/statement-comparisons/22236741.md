\begin{abstract}
Composite biodiversity scores compress heterogeneous evidence into one decision
surface, making their governance as consequential as their arithmetic. We study
a deliberately narrow question: after component definitions, normalization,
and observations are frozen, how much can changing a linear score's weights
alter a pairwise project comparison? For charter weights $w^0$ and alternative
weights $w$ on the probability simplex, project contrast $z=x-y$, and total
variation $\mathrm{TV}(w,w^0)=\frac12\lVert w-w^0\rVert_1$, we obtain
\[
 |(w-w^0)\!\cdot z|\leq \mathrm{TV}(w,w^0)
 [\max_i z_i-\min_i z_i].
\]
Thus a charter margin exceeding a declared total-variation radius times the
component-contrast range cannot be rank-reversed by permitted reweighting. The
coefficient is sharp under an explicit mass-transfer condition. A preregistered
standard-library gate initially failed one convergence check; after preserving
that evidence and extending only the experiment, the final gate passed 15/15
checks. This is a generated exploration and a standard robust-aggregation
corollary, not empirical D-Score validation or a novelty claim. Its exact declared Lean statements are Comparator-certified.
\end{abstract}

\textbf{Keywords:} biodiversity indicators; composite index; D-Score;

\section{Introduction}

Biodiversity decisions increasingly rely on baskets of indicators: taxa,
abundance, habitat condition, connectivity, structure, and remotely sensed
proxies may be normalized and aggregated into one score. This compression is
useful, but it introduces choices about what counts, how components are scaled,
and how much each component matters. Recent work on emerging biodiversity
credits emphasizes that these decisions are not merely technical. Wauchope et
al. document deep measurement choices and gaming vulnerability in attempts to
construct a tradable ``unit of nature'' \cite{wauchope2024}. Wunder et al.
identify flexible monitoring and ex-post indicator choice as integrity risks
\cite{wunder2025}. Kim et al. place measurement transparency within a broader
governance architecture for high-integrity credits \cite{kim2025}.

Composite-indicator research reaches a compatible warning from another
direction. Kelemen et al. show that changing min--max scoring thresholds acts as
implicit reweighting and can alter rankings even when explicit weights are held
fixed \cite{kelemen2024}. Therefore a weight certificate can be meaningful only
after the component set, orientation, normalization, and observations are
frozen. It cannot make the surrounding metric valid by itself.

The Viridis D-Score lineage has separately examined component weighting,
finite-sample measurement closure, revisit timing, and temporal coherence. The
present Stewardship intersection isolates a governance question left between
those topics: what does a precommitted weight charter actually guarantee?

Our contribution is bounded. We apply the elementary duality between total
variation and the range of a bounded function to a pairwise composite-score
contrast. The result gives a dimension-free robustness certificate, an exact
unconstrained cherry-pick premium, and a sharpness witness. It is likely close
to standard robust-ranking and probability-measure sensitivity results.
Novelty and significance are therefore unassessed.

\section{Model and preregistered claims}

Consider $n\geq2$ frozen components. Project $X$ has normalized component vector
$x\in\mathbf{R}^n$ and project $Y$ has $y\in\mathbf{R}^n$. Their contrast is
$z=x-y$. A weight vector belongs to the simplex
\[
 \Delta_n=\{w\in\mathbf{R}^n:w_i\geq0,\ \sum_iw_i=1\},
\]
and the linear composite score is $S_w(x)=w\cdot x$. A stewardship charter
precommits to $w^0\in\Delta_n$. Later weights $w$ are allowed only when
$\mathrm{TV}(w,w^0)\leq\rho$, where
\[
 \mathrm{TV}(w,w^0)=\frac12\sum_i|w_i-w_i^0|.
\]

Before conclusions or manuscript drafting, we preregistered three hypotheses.
First, score drift is bounded by total variation times the range of $z$.
Second, a charter advantage larger than this budget prevents rank reversal.
Third, the coefficient is attainable when sufficient charter mass can be moved
from a minimum-contrast component to a maximum-contrast component. We also
precommitted to retain normalization drift and omitted indicators as negative
controls.

\section{Results}

\subsection{TV--range certificate}

\textbf{Proposition 1 (generated, proposed for formalization).} For any
$w,w^0\in\Delta_n$ and $z\in\mathbf{R}^n$,
\begin{equation}
 |S_w(z)-S_{w^0}(z)|\leq \mathrm{TV}(w,w^0)\,\mathrm{range}(z),
 \label{eq:certificate}
\end{equation}
where $\mathrm{range}(z)=\max_i z_i-\min_i z_i$.

To see the mechanism, subtract any constant $c$ from every component of $z$.
Because both weight vectors sum to one, $(w-w^0)\cdot c\mathbf{1}=0$. Choosing
$c=(\max z+\min z)/2$ gives
\[
 |(w-w^0)\cdot z|
 \leq \lVert w-w^0\rVert_1\frac{\mathrm{range}(z)}2,
\]
which is Eq.~\eqref{eq:certificate}. This derivation is mathematical prose, not
an audited Lean proof.

\textbf{Corollary 1 (rank stability).} If $\mathrm{TV}(w,w^0)\leq\rho$ and
\begin{equation}
 S_{w^0}(x)-S_{w^0}(y)>\rho\,\mathrm{range}(x-y),
 \label{eq:margin}
\end{equation}
then $S_w(x)>S_w(y)$. The strict inequality matters: equality gives no strict
ranking guarantee.

When normalized component contrasts lie in an interval of width one, the score
can move by at most $\rho$. This is dimension free; adding components cannot by
itself enlarge the bound. It may still enlarge modeling discretion, missingness,
or measurement burden, none of which Eq.~\eqref{eq:certificate} controls.

\subsection{Sharpness and ex-post selection}

Suppose index $a$ minimizes $z_i$, index $b$ maximizes it, $w_a^0\geq\rho$, and
$w_b^0\leq1-\rho$. Moving mass $\rho$ from $a$ to $b$ yields
\[
 \mathrm{TV}(w,w^0)=\rho,\qquad
 S_w(z)-S_{w^0}(z)=\rho[\max_i z_i-\min_i z_i].
\]
The coefficient in Eq.~\eqref{eq:certificate} therefore cannot be uniformly
improved under those conditions.

If no charter restriction exists, maximizing a linear score over the simplex
places all mass on a maximum component:
\begin{equation}
 \max_{w\in\Delta_n}w\cdot z=\max_i z_i.
\end{equation}
The ex-post selection premium above the charter score is exactly
$\max_i z_i-w^0\cdot z$. This value measures arithmetic discretion, not intent;
it should not by itself be described as fraud or ecological harm.

\subsection{Worked example}

Take $w^0=(0.6,0.4)$, $w=(0.3,0.7)$, and $z=(0,5)$. Total variation is $0.3$,
the contrast range is $5$, and the score rises from $2.0$ to $3.5$. The change
$1.5$ exactly equals the certificate $0.3\times5$. The example also shows why
weight drift should be reported in a geometry tied to score sensitivity rather
than as a list of percentage-point changes.

\section{Negative controls and failure boundaries}

\textbf{Normalization drift.} With weights fixed at $(1/2,1/2)$, changing the
first component's scale from $(1,2)$ to $(10,2)$ moves the score from $1.5$ to
$6.0$ while weight TV remains zero. Kelemen et al.'s empirical sensitivity
analysis demonstrates why this is not an artificial concern \cite{kelemen2024}.

\textbf{Omitted component.} A positive score over included components places no
bound on an excluded adverse outcome. Indicator precommitment must therefore
cover membership and direction, not weights alone.

\textbf{Ecological validity.} Monitoring technologies differ in accessibility,
granularity, evidenceability, and dependence on ground validation
\cite{ford2024}. A robust aggregation of biased or irrelevant components is
still biased or irrelevant.

\textbf{Causality and additionality.} The certificate compares reported
component outcomes. It does not establish that a project caused them, that a
baseline is credible, that leakage is absent, or that gains are durable.

\section{Discussion}

The practical object is not a universally correct set of weights. It is a
transparent charter: frozen components and transformations, a declared weight
vector, an allowed TV radius, and pairwise margins reported against their
worst-case drift budget. A steward can then distinguish comparisons that are
stable under the charter from those whose ordering depends on discretionary
reweighting.

This structure is intentionally weaker than a full sensitivity analysis. It
does not sample alternative models, propagate measurement uncertainty, or
resolve normative disagreements. It is complementary to those tasks. Its value
is auditability: every term in Eq.~\eqref{eq:margin} can be recomputed from a
frozen scorecard.

The result should not receive a priority claim. Total-variation duality is
standard, and rank robustness under weight variation belongs to a mature
multi-criteria decision literature. The D-Score application may still be useful
as a compact governance certificate, but that usefulness requires independent
significance review and empirical comparison with ordinary sensitivity tools.

\section{Limitations and falsifiable next tests}

The linear model permits full compensation among components. Real biodiversity
governance may require vetoes, floors, lexicographic priorities, or
non-substitutability. The component range can be dominated by an outlier.
Total-variation radius is interpretable but not automatically normatively
correct. Pairwise stability does not guarantee stability of an entire ranking
when many projects are near-tied. None of the current evidence is field data.

A next empirical test would freeze a real scorecard before revealing held-out
project outcomes, compare the certificate with Monte Carlo and scenario-based
sensitivity analysis, and test whether flagged comparisons predict later
ranking disputes or revisions. Refutation occurs if certified comparisons
reverse without a charter violation, or if non-certified comparisons are no
more dispute-prone than certified ones across a preregistered evaluation set.
