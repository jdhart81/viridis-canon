\section{Introduction}
Replacement and retrofit decisions couple physical burdens to capital timing. Existing work treats technological obsolescence, heterogeneous capital turnover, carbon abatement timing, and product replacement under technological progress \cite{nair1992,jaccard2007,chenavaz2025}. Here the narrower goal is auditability: once a decision maker declares a horizon, shadow-price path, avoided intensity, embodied transition burden, capital price, and smooth transition friction, what allocation follows from one explicit objective?

The contribution is not a general replacement theory. It is a compact bridge from thermodynamic burden accounting to a standard separable resource-allocation calculation. That limited role matters because a physically labeled objective does not by itself validate its prices, measurements, institutional authority, or causal effect.

\section{Model and benefit ledger}
For asset $i$, let $p_i(t)$ be a declared price per unit burden, $\Delta e_i(t)$ the avoided burden rate under complete retrofit, $r\geq0$ the discount rate, and $s_i\geq0$ an embodied transition burden expressed in the same objective units. Define
\begin{equation}
b_i=\int_0^H e^{-rt}p_i(t)\Delta e_i(t)\,dt-s_i.
\end{equation}
For the test family $p_i(t)=p_{i0}e^{g_it}$ and constant $\Delta e_i$,
\begin{equation}
b_i=p_{i0}\Delta e_i\frac{e^{(g_i-r)H}-1}{g_i-r}-s_i,
\end{equation}
with the continuous limit $p_{i0}\Delta e_i H-s_i$ when $g_i=r$.

Let $x_i\in[0,1]$ be the retrofit fraction, $q_i>0$ a declared transition-friction coefficient, $c_i>0$ the capital price of full retrofit, and $B\geq0$ the shared budget. The audit program is
\begin{equation}
\max_x\; \Phi(x)=\sum_i\left(b_ix_i-\frac{q_i}{2}x_i^2\right)
\quad\text{s.t.}\quad \sum_i c_ix_i\leq B,\quad 0\leq x_i\leq1.
\end{equation}
The quadratic term is a modeling choice representing rising marginal disruption; omitting it changes the allocation problem.

\section{Result and proof sketch}
For $z\in\mathbb{R}$ write $[z]_0^1=\min\{1,\max\{0,z\}\}$.

\paragraph{Allocation result.} The program has one maximizing allocation. There is a multiplier $\lambda\geq0$ such that
\begin{equation}
x_i(\lambda)=\left[\frac{b_i-\lambda c_i}{q_i}\right]_0^1.
\end{equation}
If $\sum_i c_i[b_i/q_i]_0^1\leq B$, then $\lambda=0$. Otherwise a positive multiplier can be chosen so that $\sum_i c_ix_i(\lambda)=B$.

The Hessian is the negative diagonal matrix $-\mathrm{diag}(q_i)$, so the objective is strictly concave. The feasible set is compact and convex, hence a maximizer exists and strict concavity fixes its allocation. The KKT stationarity conditions separate by coordinate; lower and upper normal-cone conditions produce the clipping formula. The budget response is continuous and nonincreasing in $\lambda$, is above $B$ at zero in the binding case, and vanishes for sufficiently large $\lambda$. Complementary slackness gives the two regimes. These steps, rather than the one-coordinate derivative alone, are the preregistered nontriviality scope.

\section{Predictions and computational tests}
The model predicts that larger $b_i$ raises an interior allocation, larger $c_i$ reduces it when the budget binds, and larger $q_i$ shrinks the response toward zero for fixed numerator. Increasing embodied burden lowers $b_i$. Common rescaling of all $b_i$, $q_i$, and the multiplier preserves the allocation.
