\begin{abstract}
A linear biodiversity composite can cross a stewardship threshold even when
its components remain uncertain.  For fixed nonnegative weights, nominal
components $x_i$, and supplied absolute uncertainty radii $r_i$, we record the
exact robust interval
\[
D_w(x)-\sum_i w_i r_i\le D_w(y)\le D_w(x)+\sum_i w_i r_i
\quad\text{whenever}\quad |y_i-x_i|\le r_i.
\]
The lower endpoint is attained by the simultaneous lower corner.  Thus a
nominal score protects a declared stewardship floor $\tau$ whenever its margin
is at least the weighted uncertainty reserve.  The result is elementary
interval arithmetic and is classified as a methods note.  It does not estimate
uncertainty, validate D-Score, or establish ecological outcomes.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} biodiversity indicator; composite index;
uncertainty; robust threshold; stewardship; D-Score

\section{Introduction}
D-Score currently aggregates normalized species, phylogenetic, and genetic
components by a linear weighted sum.  A displayed point score can conceal that
the components have different sampling errors, taxonomic coverage, or model
uncertainties.  Composite-indicator guidance therefore calls for explicit
uncertainty and sensitivity analysis \cite{oecd,burgass}, while biodiversity
indicator practice emphasizes matching interval form to data and purpose
\cite{nicholson}.

Run 132 treated changes in a precommitted weight charter.  Run 146 fixed the
weights and propagated one common absolute tolerance.  Here we freeze
heterogeneous component radii and ask a narrower stewardship question: what is
the exact lowest linear D-Score compatible with those supplied intervals?

\section{Related work and novelty boundary}
The OECD/JRC handbook treats uncertainty analysis as part of responsible
composite-indicator construction \cite{oecd}.  Burgass et al. classify the many
uncertainties affecting environmental composites, including structural and
deep uncertainty \cite{burgass}.  Nicholson et al. compare ways to represent
variability and uncertainty for biodiversity indicators \cite{nicholson}.
Our calculation is a standard box-uncertainty support-function identity.  We
claim neither a new robust-optimization theory nor a validated uncertainty
model.  Novelty and significance remain unassessed.

\section{Model and assumptions}
Let $w=(w_S,w_P,w_G)$ be fixed with $w_i\ge0$, and define
\[
D_w(x)=w_Sx_S+w_Px_P+w_Gx_G.
\]
For nominal $x$ and supplied radii $r_i\ge0$, an admissible realization $y$
satisfies $|y_i-x_i|\le r_i$.  Define the reserve
\[
R_w(r)=w_Sr_S+w_Pr_P+w_Gr_G.
\]
The components and radii must use compatible normalized-score units.
Nonnegative weights and valid component intervals are load-bearing.  Weight
normalization is needed for the conventional convex-composite interpretation,
but not for the algebraic interval identity.

\section{Main result}
From $-r_i\le y_i-x_i\le r_i$ and $w_i\ge0$,
\[
-w_ir_i\le w_i(y_i-x_i)\le w_ir_i.
\]
Summing gives
\begin{equation}
-R_w(r)\le D_w(y)-D_w(x)\le R_w(r).\label{eq:interval}
\end{equation}
The lower and upper endpoints are attained at $y=x-r$ and $y=x+r$,
respectively.  Hence the lower score
$L_w(x,r)=D_w(x)-R_w(r)$ is the exact minimum over the declared box.

For a precommitted floor $\tau$, the sufficient and sharp box-robust rule is
\begin{equation}
\tau+R_w(r)\le D_w(x)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\tau\le D_w(y)\text{ for every admissible }y.\label{eq:floor}
\end{equation}
Increasing a radius cannot reduce $R_w(r)$; a zero-weight component contributes
nothing.  With weights $(1/2,3/10,1/5)$, nominal components
$(4/5,7/10,3/5)$, and radii $(1/10,1/5,1/20)$, the nominal score is $73/100$,
the reserve is $12/100$, and the exact lower score is $61/100$.

\section{Discussion}
The reserve is an audit aid, not a license to select convenient uncertainty.
It makes the cost of heterogeneous intervals visible in the same units as the
linear score.  A steward can report the nominal score, the weighted reserve,
and the robust lower score separately.  Doing so prevents a point estimate
from silently carrying more decision authority than its declared component
evidence supports.

The additive score remains compensatory: a high component can offset a low
one.  If a stewardship charter forbids such substitution, a minimum, veto,
geometric, or other noncompensatory rule must be specified and analyzed
separately.

\section{Limitations}
No ecological dataset is analyzed.  The radii are supplied rather than
estimated and do not model correlation, systematic bias, temporal mismatch,
missingness, structural uncertainty, or threshold-selection error.  Simultaneous
corner attainment may be conservative when dependencies exclude that corner.
The note does not validate D-Score normalization or weights, justify a policy
threshold, establish additionality, or infer conservation impact.
