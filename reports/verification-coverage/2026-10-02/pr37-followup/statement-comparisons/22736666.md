\begin{abstract}
We isolate a two-partner linear mutualism and derive its exact feasibility
wedge.  With intrinsic inputs $r_A,r_B$, reciprocal gains $m,n\geq0$, and
loop gain $mn<1$, the equilibrium is
$x=(r_A+m r_B)/(1-mn)$ and $y=(r_B+n r_A)/(1-mn)$.  The numerator signs are
therefore necessary and sufficient for positivity.  One declining partner can
be rescued by a positive partner only when helpful coupling exceeds its deficit
while feedback burden remains below the donor's intrinsic margin.  If both
intrinsic inputs are nonpositive, stable reciprocal amplification cannot create
a positive equilibrium.  Exact-rational tests support the algebra; the seven frozen Lean declarations now have an exact-hash private

\section{Model and preregistered claims}
Consider the fixed-point equations
\begin{equation}
x=r_A+m y,\qquad y=r_B+n x,
\end{equation}
where $m,n\geq0$ are supplied reciprocal gains.  We assume $mn<1$; this is a
bounded-loop condition for this linear model, not a general ecological
stability theorem.  Solving gives
\begin{equation}
x=\frac{r_A+m r_B}{1-mn},\qquad
y=\frac{r_B+n r_A}{1-mn}.\label{eq:solution}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Proposition 1 (feasibility signs).}
Under $mn<1$, $x>0$ exactly when $r_A+m r_B>0$, and $y>0$ exactly when
$r_B+n r_A>0$.  This follows because the common denominator in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:solution} is positive.

\paragraph{Proposition 2 (one-sided rescue window).}
If $r_A<0<r_B$, then the supplied equilibrium is positive whenever
\begin{equation}
-r_A<m r_B,\qquad n(-r_A)<r_B,\qquad mn<1.\label{eq:wedge}
\end{equation}
The first inequality is the minimum helpful coupling needed to lift partner A;
the second is the maximum feedback burden compatible with keeping B positive.
For $r_A=-1,r_B=3,m=1/2,n=1/4$, the denominator is $7/8$ and
$(x,y)=(4/7,22/7)$, so the wedge is nonempty.

\paragraph{Proposition 3 (no two-decliner rescue).}
If $r_A\leq0$, $r_B\leq0$, and $m,n\geq0$, both numerators in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:solution} are nonpositive.  Under $mn<1$, neither equilibrium
coordinate can be positive.  Reciprocal amplification redistributes or
amplifies positive input; it does not manufacture it in this model.
