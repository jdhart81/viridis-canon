\begin{abstract}
We isolate a fixed-overhead identity for two ecosystem-service claims. If a
verification setup costs $F$ and claim-specific burdens are $v_1,v_2$, two
separate audits cost $E_{\rm sep}=(F+v_1)+(F+v_2)$, while one shared audit costs
$E_{\rm bundle}=F+v_1+v_2$. The no-interaction saving is exactly $F$. With
bundle-specific interaction overhead $h$, the saving becomes $F-h$, so sharing
is cheaper only when $h<F$. A supplied per-bit lower bound propagates through
the same ledger. Exact-rational tests support the identities; the eight frozen Lean declarations now have private dual-kernel Comparator
certification bound to the exact proof bytes. This is a methods note, not evidence that ecosystem services are
additive, credits may be stacked, or a real MRV system saves energy or money.
\end{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} ecosystem services; verification overhead; MRV;
transaction costs; Landauer bound; methods note

\section{Introduction and prior work}
Ecosystem-service bundles are empirical patterns of services that recur across
space or time and may contain both synergies and trade-offs
\cite{raudsepp2010}. A shared label therefore does not make service quantities
additive. Separately, transaction costs materially affect participation and
performance in payment-for-ecosystem-services coordination experiments
\cite{banerjee2017}. These findings motivate transparent cost ledgers but do not
imply that any two claims can share one audit.

At the physical limit, Landauer associated logically irreversible information
processing with a minimum heat cost \cite{landauer1961}. We use this only as a
conditional interpretation of a supplied per-bit floor. No device, registry,
sensor, or computation is measured here.

Earlier Viridis work studied verification-market phase boundaries (Run 081),
mutualistic attestation (Run 100), and response-adjusted service alignment
(Run 134). The present object is narrower: a two-claim arithmetic ledger for
fixed, marginal, and interaction burdens. Novelty and significance remain
unassessed.

\section{Model and preregistered claims}
Let $F,v_1,v_2\geq0$ be commensurate verification burdens. Separate audits and
a shared audit are
\begin{align}
 E_{\rm sep} &= (F+v_1)+(F+v_2),\\
 E_{\rm bundle} &= F+v_1+v_2.
\end{align}

\paragraph{Proposition 1 (fixed-overhead saving).}
Direct expansion gives
\begin{equation}
 E_{\rm sep}-E_{\rm bundle}=F. \label{eq:saving}
\end{equation}
This is an accounting identity under identical setup and unchanged marginal
burdens. It is not an empirical economy-of-scope estimate.

\paragraph{Proposition 2 (interaction correction).}
If a shared audit requires extra interaction work $h\geq0$, define
$E_{\rm bundle}^{h}=F+v_1+v_2+h$. Then
\begin{equation}
 E_{\rm sep}-E_{\rm bundle}^{h}=F-h.
\end{equation}
Hence sharing is strictly cheaper when $h<F$, breaks even at $h=F$, and is more
burdensome when $h>F$. The last regime is the preregistered negative control.

\paragraph{Proposition 3 (conditional marginal floor).}
Suppose each claim processes $b_i$ logically irreversible bits in a specified
implementation and $v_i\geq\epsilon b_i$. Then
\begin{equation}
 E_{\rm bundle}\geq F+\epsilon(b_1+b_2). \label{eq:floor}
\end{equation}
For an isothermal idealization one may set $\epsilon=k_B T\ln2$, but only when
the physical and logical assumptions of that interpretation are established.

\paragraph{Proposition 4 (net-feasibility bookkeeping).}
For supplied gross service value $G$ in the same ledger unit, define
$N=G-E_{\rm bundle}$. Then $N\geq0$ exactly when
$E_{\rm bundle}\leq G$. This is not a welfare, pricing, or issuance theorem.

The instance $(F,v_1,v_2)=(2,3,5)$ gives $E_{\rm sep}=12$,
$E_{\rm bundle}=10$, and saving $2$, establishing nonvacuity.

\section{Verification and negative controls}
The standard-library verifier uses exact rational arithmetic. It exhausts a
nonnegative quarter-grid for fixed and marginal burdens, checks the expansions,
fixed saving, nonnegative boundary, and the gross-service feasibility
equivalence. A second grid varies interaction overhead through regimes below,
at, and above $F$. A third grid propagates supplied marginal per-bit lower
bounds. No tolerance, random seed, external data, or network access is used.

The eight named Lean declarations cover the two cost expansions, fixed and

\section{Discussion, falsification, and limitations}
Equation~\eqref{eq:saving} is useful only after the ledger boundaries are
defined. Real projects may have different setup costs, correlated errors,
service interactions, additionality and permanence requirements, distinct
spatial scales, legal constraints, or anti-double-counting work. Any of these
can change $v_i$ or add $h$; omitting them manufactures a saving.

The note excludes ecological production functions, causal attribution,
uncertainty calibration, registry reconciliation, credit fungibility,
additionality, permanence, leakage, welfare, prices, customer behavior, and
device-level thermodynamics. It is falsified by an exact-rational counterexample
under its assumptions or any paper-to-Lean statement mismatch. Empirical use
would additionally require measured cost components and an audit demonstrating
that setup is genuinely shared without weakening claim-specific evidence.
