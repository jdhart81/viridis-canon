\begin{abstract}
Thermodynamic descriptions of learning require optimization gain and physical dissipation to remain distinct. We isolate a minimal one-step audit. For a supplied gain scale $a\ge0$, balance step $q\ge0$, and update step $\eta$, define the local surrogate $G(\eta)=a\eta(2q-\eta)$. Then $G(q)-G(\eta)=a(\eta-q)^2$, so the gain is nonnegative on $0\le\eta\le2q$, has a (possibly nonunique) global maximum at $\eta=q$, and is symmetric under equal mistuning around the peak. A separately supplied heat proxy $H(\eta)=d\eta^2$ scales quadratically. The result is elementary bookkeeping. It does not derive a physical law, calibrate $a,q,d$, validate an optimizer, or establish energy savings.
\end{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} entropy-driven learning; thermodynamics of learning; learning rate; quadratic surrogate; dissipation proxy.

\section{Introduction and prior work}
Stochastic thermodynamics has been used to bound information acquired by a model neural network in terms of thermodynamic cost \cite{goldt2017}. Constant-step stochastic gradient descent can also be analyzed as a stochastic process with an approximate stationary distribution \cite{mandt2017}. More recently, tempering has been connected to entropic mirror descent in Fisher--Rao geometry \cite{chopin2024}. Those results are substantially richer than this note and make a novelty claim unsafe.

The present question is deliberately smaller: if an operator declares a local concave quadratic gain surrogate and a separate quadratic heat proxy, what exact corridor can be audited before interpreting either as a real learner? The answer is a completed square. Keeping the two ledgers separate prevents a numerical optimization identity from being reported as a measured thermodynamic efficiency.

\section{Model and preregistered claim}
Let $a\ge0$ be a supplied gain scale, $q\ge0$ a supplied balance step, and $\eta\in\mathbb{R}$ a scalar update step. Define
\begin{equation}
G(\eta)=a\eta(2q-\eta). \label{eq:gain}
\end{equation}
For a separately supplied coefficient $d\ge0$, define the heat proxy
\begin{equation}
H(\eta)=d\eta^2. \label{eq:heat}
\end{equation}
The symbols are conditional model inputs. No conversion from $G$ to joules is assumed.

\paragraph{Triviality probe.} Expansion and factor nonnegativity prove the load-bearing result in at most five lines, so the disposition is fixed to \texttt{METHODS\_NOTE}.

\begin{proposition}[Thermal learning-rate corridor]
For every real $\eta$,
\begin{equation}
G(q)-G(\eta)=a(\eta-q)^2. \label{eq:deficit}
\end{equation}
Consequently $G(\eta)\le G(q)=aq^2$. If $0\le\eta\le2q$, then $G(\eta)\ge0$. For every $\delta\in\mathbb{R}$, $G(q-\delta)=G(q+\delta)$, and for every scale $k$, $H(k\eta)=k^2H(\eta)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Expanding Eq.~\eqref{eq:deficit} gives the square identity. Its right side is nonnegative. Inside the stated corridor the factors $a$, $\eta$, and $2q-\eta$ are nonnegative. Symmetry and heat scaling follow by substitution.
\end{proof}

At $(a,q,\eta,d)=(2,3,3,1)$, gain is $18$ and the heat proxy is $9$. At $(1,2,5,1)$, the step overshoots the sign-safe corridor, giving gain $-5$ and heat proxy $25$.

\section{Discussion, limitations, and falsifiability}
Equation~\eqref{eq:gain} resembles the decrease certificate obtained by applying a smoothness upper bound to a one-dimensional gradient step, but this note does not establish that derivation for any particular loss. The balance step $q$ is not an observed optimal learning rate, $a$ is not predictive information, and $d$ is not a measured friction or device-energy coefficient. Stochastic gradients, curvature drift, momentum, adaptive preconditioning, minibatching, hardware state, wall-clock time, and thermal coupling are absent.

A counterexample to any exact identity, a negative in-corridor gain under the declared signs, or nonreproducible exact-rational output would falsify the mathematical package. Evidence that a real learner violates the assumed quadratic surrogate invalidates the model's applicability rather than the algebra.
