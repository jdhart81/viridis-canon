\begin{abstract}
Stewardship proposals sometimes combine spendable funds with balances described as protected reserves. We isolate one deliberately elementary audit invariant. For nonnegative integer balances, if spending $x$ is bounded by the spendable account $a$, then adjoining the same ring-fenced reserve $r$ preserves the bound: $x+r\le a+r$. Zero spendable balance forces zero spending under the premise, and increasing the declared reserve cannot reduce the gross balance. Exhaustive exact-integer checks accompany four frozen Lean statements. The result is a methods note about bookkeeping only: it does not show fiduciary compliance, conservation delivery, investment performance, optimal allocation, or ecological impact.
\end{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} stewardship; conservation finance; reserve accounting; natural capital; auditability

\section{Introduction and prior work}
Natural-capital trusts have been proposed to protect intergenerational assets and route depletion rents toward conservation \cite{helm2019}. Ecological accounting work likewise describes natural-capital reserves set aside from trading \cite{gann2021}. Modern biodiversity-finance research studies private and blended financing across return, risk, and biodiversity-impact dimensions \cite{garel2025}. Those works address institutions, ecological units, and financing structures. This note proves none of their substantive claims. It asks only what a ledger must preserve after the analyst has already declared which units are spendable and which are ring-fenced.

The core argument is a standard one-line monotonicity fact. The pre-registered triviality probe therefore assigns \texttt{METHODS\_NOTE}; no novelty, policy, or optimality claim is made.

\section{Model and result}
Let $a,r,x\in\mathbb{N}$ denote spendable units, reserved units, and units spent in one reporting period. Assume
\begin{equation} x\le a. \label{eq:premise}\end{equation}
Adding the same reserve to both sides gives
\begin{equation} x+r\le a+r. \label{eq:preserved}\end{equation}
This does not authorize spending from $r$; it merely states that the gross ledger remains ordered when the protected balance is reported separately. If $a=0$, Equation~\ref{eq:premise} forces $x=0$. For $r_1\le r_2$, monotonicity gives $x+r_1\le x+r_2$. The witness $(x,a,r)=(4,7,6)$ is positive and nonvacuous.

\section{Discussion and limitations}
The model assumes units are nonnegative, commensurate, correctly classified, and observed without error. It omits time, discounting, uncertainty, leakage, legal enforceability, governance capture, investment returns, administrative cost, additionality, and ecological measurement. A real reserve can be mislabeled, diverted, impaired, or economically inadequate even when the arithmetic passes.
